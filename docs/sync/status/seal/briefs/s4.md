## Build

Fill every `unimplemented!()` in `file.rs` and `backup.rs`:

1. Sealed files (§19): `StorageKey`, `K_file(kind)` (§6.6), `FileSealer::create` (draws the `file_id`,
   returns the 55-byte header), `resume`, `seal_record` (record `i` under `AD(i)`, a fresh nonce each),
   `open_file` with the steps of §19.4 in order: the header checks, the torn tail, a record that does
   not open as `seal/file_corrupt` when bytes follow it and as a bad tail when it is the last. Each
   `ct_len` is checked against its limit and the bytes remaining before anything is allocated. Record
   plaintexts come back in zeroizing buffers.
2. History records (§21.5): `HistoryRecord::from_accepted` (refuses a kind other than `ctl` with
   `seal/not_control`), `to_bytes`, `from_bytes` (anything that is not exactly one record is
   `seal/backup_history`), `seq`.
3. The projection (§21.6 "Projection"): a private function over history records and a board id that
   runs the six steps in order and returns the reduced membership, each failure `seal/backup_history`.
   Task S5's `open_snapshot` calls it with the receiver's own board id: document it at its definition.
4. `write_backup` (§21.2 to §21.5): refuses with `seal/backup_unpinned` when the membership's current
   recovery key is not the pinned one; raw DEFLATE of the payload; HPKE base mode to the recovery key
   with the `info` and `aad` of §21.3 (the ephemeral key as `wrap_root` makes it: 32 drawn bytes,
   `DeriveKeyPair`); the signature of §16.3. It keeps and returns nothing that opens the file
   (SEAL-BKP-007).
5. `read_backup` (§21.6): the eleven steps in order, the file rejected at the first that fails; nothing
   of the payload beyond the history is parsed before the writer has passed step 10 (SEAL-BKP-005);
   inflate while streaming under the 512 MiB cap, never allocating a claimed size, failing on more
   output or on input after the stream's end; every root checked against its commitment; the state
   hash of a nightly file recomputed; a segment's chain recomputed from `chain_before`.
6. `verify_backup_file`: steps 1, 2 and 5 only, and the header's `writer_sign_pk` equal to the given
   key.

## Rulings after the implementation (for the verify and security passes)

The implementer raised eleven spec questions. The lead's rulings; judge the code against them:

- `seal_record` refuses a record over the limit of §19.1 with `seal/payload_too_large`, before it draws.
- `open_file` returns `seal/file_corrupt` alone for a record that fails to open with bytes after it:
  the records before it are not returned (the API's `Result` has no place for both).
- `HistoryRecord::from_accepted` gives `seal/frame_format` for a frame whose header does not parse and
  `seal/bad_payload` for a control payload that is not §11.3's.
- `write_backup` refuses inconsistent inputs with the code of the reader's step that would reject the
  file (`seal/backup_history`, `seal/backup_writer`, `seal/backup_payload`; `seal/membership_format`).
- A segment carries only roots whose commitment its membership (`M_{first_seq − 1}`) records: the roots
  of epochs rotated inside the segment are in those rotations' wraps to the recovery key. The writer
  refuses, and the reader rejects, a root without a commitment.
- The segment's chain is recomputed frame by frame; the crate has nothing to compare it with (the
  caller chains segments and nightly files). Each frame is at most 262,144 bytes.
- A `hash_version` the membership's rules do not allow is `seal/backup_payload`.
- A history record after the file's `seq` fails step 9; one at or after a segment's `first_seq` fails
  step 11; so a segment has `first_seq ≥ 2`.
- The projection parses the first record's items at step 2, to learn the Genesis key; step 6 binds
  that key to the trusted board id.
- The projection runs the whole rows of §13.6 for `DeviceAdded`, `DeviceRevoked`, `DeviceReinstated`
  and `EpochRotated`, and `RecoveryKeyChange` without the 72 hours; `Fence`, `ResetTo`, `Skip` and
  `RulesUpgrade` check their author only. It must accept every history an honest log produces: the
  verifier checks that with histories from the board simulator that use every transition.
- `verify_backup_file` compares the header's key after steps 1 and 2 and before the signature.
