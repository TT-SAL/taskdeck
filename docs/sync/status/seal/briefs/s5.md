Lead rulings:

- The public API is seal.md §26 and nothing else (decision L9).
- §20.5 step 7 compares "S and the state hash" with expected values, but `open_snapshot` receives only
  `s` and a `BlobRef`, which has no state hash. Take this reading and list it as a spec question:
  `open_snapshot` checks that the body's `S` is `s` and that the body's own `state_hash` is the state
  hash of its `board` and `membership` (`state_hash` of §26); comparing that value with the signed
  entry's is the caller's, who holds the entry.
- Tests build large inputs without committing them: nothing over a few hundred kilobytes is written
  to the repository.

## Tests and vectors

- Each step of §20.5 fails once, in order, with every earlier step passing, for a snapshot and for a
  blob of kind 2; the declared mode's differences; a decompression bomb (a small sealed snapshot whose
  stream inflates past `inflated`) is stopped at the cap; a history that fails the projection, and one
  that disagrees with the membership, are `seal/blob_open`; parts of the wrong sizes or count.
- `tamper`: flipping any byte of the sealed bytes fails at the hash (and, with the hash recomputed to
  match, at the header or the AEAD). `contexts`: a blob does not open as an entry, a file record or the
  other blob kind; a snapshot sealed for one `S`, writer, board or epoch does not open for another
  (SEAL-CTX-003, V1-12). `parsers`: the three openers over random and mutated parts.
- Vectors: `seal-blob.json` (§24's table).
