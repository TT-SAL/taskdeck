## Part 1: carried over from task S4's security passes (do these first)

Task S4's two security passes reproduced the following; the lead ruled on each. Each gets a test that
fails without the change.

1. **The projection must not copy the membership for every record.** `project` does `m.dup()` per
   history record, so its time grows with the square of the history: a hostile backup at the limit of
   1,048,576 records (an 80 MB file that any device that ever was a member can write) held
   `read_backup` for 516 seconds in a release build, and `open_snapshot` will call the same function.
   A record that fails ends the projection, so nothing needs to be undone: apply each record's items
   to the projection itself. Then read every step once more for anything else whose cost per record
   grows with the number of records so far, and say in your report what you found. The test must be
   deterministic, with no timing: it fails when a projection copies the membership per record (for
   example a copy counter that exists only under `cfg(test)`).
2. **The first record's `ctr` must be 1** (SEAL-MEM-004; ruling Q46, now step 3 of the projection in
   §21.6): the projection accepts no
   history a log cannot produce. `seal/backup_history`. The reproduction: the Genesis record re-signed
   by the real recovery key with `ctr` 7.
3. **Step 10 of `read_backup` compares the keys, not only `dev_R`** (ruling Q45; §21.6 step 10 and
   SEAL-BKP-004 say so since the lead's spec change of 2026-11-23): `dev_R` binds the recovery signing key alone, so a history whose last recovery-key
   change pairs the sheet's signing key with another HPKE key passed, and the state a restore adopts
   would wrap every later root to that other key. Step 10 also requires both public keys of the
   projection's current recovery record to equal those of the typed seed: `seal/backup_writer`. The
   reproduction: two devices propose and confirm a recovery key made of a new sheet's signing key and
   a third party's HPKE key; a current device seals a file to the new sheet's real HPKE key; reading
   it with the new sheet's seed must fail. Add the case to `seal-backup.json` as well.
4. **Epoch roots must not pass through a state the crate cannot erase** (ruling Q48; SEAL-GEN-006, as
   task S1's high finding ruled for the hash states; §21.3 now says how). `write_backup` runs the whole payload through
   the DEFLATE compressor, whose private buffers keep its bytes, the roots and the read token among
   them, and are freed without being wiped; `unsafe` is forbidden, so the crate cannot reach them.
   - *Writer.* Keep the bytes from `n_roots` to the end of `read_token` (§21.5) out of the
     compressor. A raw DEFLATE stream is a sequence of blocks, so write it in three pieces: the bytes
     before `n_roots` through the compressor, ended with a sync or full flush (which ends on a byte
     boundary and sets no final bit); then the secret bytes in stored blocks the crate writes itself
     (RFC 1951 §3.2.4: a byte `0x00` for a block that is not final, `LEN` and its complement as two
     little-endian u16, then at most 65,535 bytes as they are); then the rest (`meta`) through a
     fresh compressor, finished, so that its last block carries the final bit. The secret bytes stay
     in zeroizing buffers. Every reader inflates this as one stream; check that with `inflate_raw`
     for payloads of many sizes (also sizes at which a flush ends exactly at the working buffer's
     end, no roots, the largest count your tests can afford) and say in your report how you ended the
     flush loop. Read the pinned `flate2` and `miniz_oxide` sources for what a flush guarantees
     (`~/.cargo/registry/src/*/flate2-1.1.9`, `miniz_oxide-0.8.9`); do not rely on memory.
   - *Reader.* `inflate_raw` resets its decompressor before dropping it, on every path, failures
     included: in the pinned version a reset zeroes the 32 KiB window that holds the last bytes
     inflated (read `miniz_oxide`'s `inflate/stream.rs` to confirm, and keep the compiler from
     dropping the write, for example with `core::hint::black_box`).
   - Write at both places, in a comment, what stays outside the crate's reach.
   - The vectors of `seal-backup.json` whose files `write_backup` produces change; regenerate them.
     A file is still "raw DEFLATE of the payload" (§21.3), and every existing reading case must pass
     unchanged.

## Part 2: blobs and snapshots

## Build

Fill every `unimplemented!()` in `blob.rs`:

1. `seal_blob` and `seal_snapshot` (§20.1 to §20.4): the `blob_id` is the first value drawn, the nonce
   the second, nothing else (SEAL-BLOB-010); the header and AD of §20.2; the padded body of §20.3; the
   snapshot body of §20.4 with one history record per seq of the membership's `ctl_seqs`, compressed
   with raw DEFLATE; `seal/snapshot_large` and no bytes when the body is over 67,108,864 bytes before
   compression or its sealed blob would be (SEAL-BLOB-009). `split_parts`.
2. `open_blob` and `open_snapshot` (§20.5): the seven steps in order, each with its code; the SHA-256
   of the concatenated parts compared in constant time before anything is decrypted; inflating under
   a cap equal to the signed `inflated` value while streaming, failing on more output, on a shorter
   output and on input after the stream's end; the history verified by task S4's projection with the
   receiver's own board id and compared with the membership as step 7 states.
3. `open_snapshot_declared` (§20.5, the declared mode): its differences one by one; the key only from
   a root the caller holds for the epoch in the sealed header (`seal/missing_root` otherwise); the
   AEAD opened before any field of the body is parsed; the 67,108,864-byte cap.

Lead rulings:

- The public API is seal.md §26 and nothing else (decision L9).
- `seal_snapshot` refuses a body its reader would reject, as `write_backup` does (ruling Q37): bytes
  in `membership` that do not parse are `seal/membership_format`; a `history` whose seqs are not
  exactly the membership's `ctl_seqs`, in order, is `seal/blob_open` (SEAL-BLOB-006 is then a clause
  the crate enforces). It does not run the projection: that is the reader's.
- A snapshot body holds no key, so `deflate_raw` as it stands is right for it.
- §20.5 step 7 compares "S and the state hash" with expected values, but `open_snapshot` receives only
  `s` and a `BlobRef`, which has no state hash. Take this reading and list it as a spec question:
  `open_snapshot` checks that the body's `S` is `s` and that the body's own `state_hash` is the state
  hash of its `board` and `membership` (`state_hash` of §26); comparing that value with the signed
  entry's is the caller's, who holds the entry.
- Tests build large inputs without committing them: nothing over a few hundred kilobytes is written
  to the repository.
